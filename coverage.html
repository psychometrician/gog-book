Appendix F — Coverage, both directions

The phrasebook answers “how do I spell this in gog”. This appendix answers the question underneath it: can it be spelled. Every entry in ggplot2’s reference index was read against the kernel. The result is two lists of very different lengths, and a table of differences between them.

Each table below has a ggplot2 column, and it is dated on purpose, because nothing here can check it: reference index of ggplot2 4.0.3, read 2026-07-26 (Wickham et al., 2026). ggplot2 is deliberately not a dependency of this book, so building the book never runs it. The plots on this page, and the one refusal shown further down, are different: the engine draws them each time the book is built.

The first list is short: what gog does that ggplot2 does not. The second is long: what ggplot2 draws that gog does not. Each gap on it is one of four kinds, and three of the four were decided on purpose. A later section turns to ggplot2’s extension packages, which the reading of the index did not include, and draws five of their plots from tables the host language computes.

F.1 What gog does that ggplot2 does not

The first section below shows a picture ggplot2 cannot draw. The other two show differences in vocabulary, not in what can be drawn.

  z(col.elevation), color(col.elevation), palette("plasma"),

“Given Maunga Whau: a surface, x is east, y is north, z is elevation, color by elevation.”

Six marks draw in the cube: point, path, bar, interval, box and surface. space(turn = , tilt = ) sets the viewing angle. facet and play split a cube’s rows as they split any other plot’s: | facet(g) gives one projected box per panel, + play(t) runs the sequence inside a single box. ggplot2 has no depth position, and no coordinate system that would read one. Its own book says it does not support true 3-D surfaces (Wickham, 2016). In R these plots come from plotly, rgl or lattice, each with a grammar of its own. Here z is one more channel in the same sentence, and Space is the chapter.

F.1.2 Texture, a channel of its own

  color(:species) + pattern(:species) + title("Hue and hatch, one legend")

  y(col.petal_length), color(col.species), pattern(col.species),
  title("Hue and hatch, one legend"))

“Given the iris flowers: bars derived by mean, x is species, y is petal length, color by species, pattern by species.”

The plot above stays legible in grayscale and for readers with color blindness, because hue and hatch both show the species. ggplot2 can fill a bar with a hatch too. There, you build each hatch yourself with R’s grid package, and it replaces the fill color. In gog, pattern is a channel of its own, separate from color. So hue and hatch can come from two different columns. On a line, the same word draws dashes, where ggplot2 needs a second word, linetype.

An area fills from its curve to its category’s center line, not to a second boundary. So area * density + x(continent) + y(life) draws half of each violin: the half violin. That is the purpose of orthogonality: gog draws the plot without adding a word to its vocabulary.

Those are all the differences in this direction. What the extensions add shows more plots that ggplot2 draws only with an extension package. Everything else gog draws, ggplot2 draws too, usually with more settings.

F.2 What is different rather than missing

The rest is not about pictures at all. These rows are differences in how the two packages work: each one follows from what its own package was built for.

ggplot2 gog
Where it runs R R, Python, Julia and JavaScript, one engine, the same SVG out of each
What a plot is R closures, alive in the session an engine-neutral JSON specification you can store, compare, send or generate
A sentence the package cannot draw a warning, and a plot with those rows removed (Removed 12 rows containing missing values) no plot, and a message naming the sentence it would accept
What combines with what documented per geom one rule table, every pair decided, dumped from the engine into Combinations
Picking out a subset with the pointer outside the package: the plot is a still image, and the answer is plotly or shiny brush is a word in the sentence, so the selection appears in the PDF as well as on screen. It dims what you did not pick and removes no rows. Selection is the chapter
Moving overlapping labels apart ggrepel, a separate package, with a geom of its own to use instead text * repel, a collision modifier beside dodge, stack and jitter, so it composes with the text mark that was already there. It draws a thin line back to the point when a label has moved far, and where no arrangement fits, it still draws every label and reports how many overlap
Spellings per plot often two (geom_bar/geom_col, geom_histogram/stat_bin) one
Saying “log” scale_x_log10(), a scale function per transformation x(gdp, scale = "log"), written on the binding
Non-positive values on a log axis dropped, with a warning refused, with the fix named

The middle of a domain is a midpoint, so no parameter is added, and the ramp is linear on each side of the middle.

F.3 What ggplot2 draws that gog does not

The gaps below are sorted into four tables by reason, because the reason tells you whether a gap is final. A gap with a written ruling behind it is a different thing from a gap nobody has ruled on.

F.3.1 Refused, with the ruling on record

gog will not draw these. Each has a written ruling, and the ruling names the direction to go instead. Design laws is where the laws behind them live.

In ggplot2 The ruling
sec_axis(), dup_axis() A second y-axis is two coordinate spaces drawn in one panel. Facet on the measure, or layer if the units really are shared.
annotate() at page coordinates, annotation_custom(), annotation_raster() A shape at pixel (120, 340) is a drawing command, and the specification has to mean the same thing at another size, in a facet, or on a sphere. Annotation in data space needs no new word: layer a mark over a small table.
ggproto(), user-written geoms and stats Every promise on the Combinations grid depends on the vocabulary being fixed. An atom missing from the rule table has no row on that grid, breaks No Exceptions, and cannot be written into the JSON. Compose instead.
coord_flip() Nothing to write: orientation is read from which axis carries the categories, so swapping the two bindings turns the plot sideways. ggplot2 has since superseded it.
geom_bar() beside geom_col(); geom_histogram() beside stat_bin(geom = "bar") gog spells each of these one way. bar draws your numbers, bar * count counts them, bar * bin bins them.
geom_smooth(method = "lm") A family of fits behind one argument is exactly the enumeration this grammar refuses. A least-squares line is a different statistic from a local smoother, so it needs its own plain name.
scale_x_log10(), scale_x_sqrt() as names One parameter derives the family: base = 2, base = 10, base = exp(1).
coord_polar(theta = "y") Which axis is the angle is read from the bindings, not chosen by an argument.
qplot(), and plotting bare vectors A plot is a mapping from a table. Put the vectors in one.
geom_vline(), geom_hline() as separate marks Three marks for one idea. They are one rule, which takes a column, so a table of thresholds draws all of them at once, and which is also the rug via style(reach = "edge").
plotmath, label_parsed(), label_bquote() A TeX engine belongs to one output format, not to the grammar. Unicode covers what readers need: superscript powers on log ticks, Greek, sub- and superscripts in a label.
coord_cartesian(xlim = ) This ggplot2 name covers two different operations. Narrowing the domain and running the statistics again is limits, which already reports how many rows it removed. Magnifying the finished picture changes how you look at it and not what the plot claims. So magnifying needs no word. Under every plot in the web edition, − and + magnify the picture, and the frame button returns to the whole of it.
plotly-style interaction: click a point, and read what it is This needs no word in the kernel, and the survey of what other tools do is what decided it. Plotly hands your application an event; Vega-Lite puts a predicate over named fields in the specification; D3 asks the author for a row identity, and uses it for animation rather than for selection. gog answers each of the three elsewhere. Reading a row belongs to the page a plot is shown on, not to the sentence. On a flat plot that carries brush, resting the pointer on a mark shows that row’s values. A click keeps them on the plot as a stamp. A predicate over a column is brush. A row identity is what an animation needs, and the grammar has no word for it (Animation).
a free-shape selection, drawn around some points with the pointer There is no lasso word in the kernel. Every other selection can be written down: brush(gdp, at = c(1200, 45000)) names a range, and the PDF then shows the plot that range asks for. A free shape can only be written as a long list of corners, which nobody writes in a sentence. The act was not refused, only the word: in the web edition you draw the shape with the pointer and the sentence still says brush.

F.3.2 The host language’s job

These are computations, not pictures. gog draws the table you give it. Compute the fitted value, the theoretical quantile or a model turned into a table before the plot, in R, Python, Julia or JavaScript. Those languages already have better tools for them.

In ggplot2 Where it goes
stat_ecdf() compute the cumulative shares, then step
stat_qq(), stat_qq_line() compute the theoretical quantiles, then point and line
stat_ellipse() compute the ellipse, then path
stat_quantile() fit the quantile regression, then line
stat_function(), geom_function() evaluate the function onto a grid, then line
stat_unique() de-duplicate the table
cut_interval(), cut_number(), cut_width() cut the column, or use bin
mean_cl_boot() bootstrap a lower and an upper bound, then ribbon * bounds(lower, upper). Two helpers documented beside it are gog atoms instead: mean_sdl() is deviation, and median_hilow() is range(0.025, 0.975) with point * median
fortify(), autoplot(), map_data() get the model or the map boundaries in the host, then give gog a table
a correlation matrix cor() where the data lives, reshape it long, then zone + x(var1) + y(var2) + color(r)

The table makes the split between gog and the host language look simpler than it is. gog does compute: bin cuts, density estimates, smooth fits a local regression, confidence builds an interval, deviation measures spread, and quantile cuts a distribution. The test is whether the picture can exist without the computation. A histogram has no bars until something bins. A quantile regression is a separate answer that is then drawn as a line. How the test applies to a new case is not settled.

F.3.3 Missing, with the direction already settled

gog does not draw these. Each one belongs to a family the grammar already has, and nothing about them is disputed.

In ggplot2 Here
labs(subtitle =, caption =) more of the label family; title() is the one that exists

F.3.4 Not decided either way

None of these has a ruling in either direction. Many groups gives reasons against custom tick text, but that is an argument, not a ruling.

In ggplot2 What is missing here
scale_*_continuous(breaks =, labels =) tick positions and tick text. tick_count asks for a number of ticks and the engine chooses them, so “tick at 0, 50 and 100” and relabeling a category cannot be written.
scale_y_reverse() a reversed continuous axis. x(continent) + order(continent, desc = TRUE) sorts a categorical axis from Z to A, or a declared order from last to first.
scale_x_sqrt(), coord_trans() a power scale. The log10-style naming is refused; the capability never was.
linewidth as an aesthetic a column mapped to stroke width. size can be set on a stroke for the whole layer, not mapped per row.
labs(alt = ), get_alt_text() alt text on the rendered plot
geom_label() text in a box, rather than bare text
geom_curve() a curved connector between two points; path joins them straight
after_stat(), after_scale(), stage(), and geom_count() with them sending a transform’s output to a channel rather than to a position. point * count + size(count) is refused, because the count a transform invented is not a column the sentence can name, so sizing a point by how many rows landed on it has no spelling. In ggplot2, all four functions in this row do this one job, and it is the largest gap on this list.
geom_contour() over a supplied z iso-lines of a field you computed. path * density traces a field gog estimated.
aes(angle = ) on geom_text(), and ggplot2’s polar text generally a separate angle for each label. style(angle = ) turns every label in a layer by the same angle, so a sunburst cannot turn each label along its own arc, and the labels of small wedges overlap. Every plot in polar has this gap, not only the sunburst.

F.4 What the extensions add

Every table above compares gog with ggplot2 itself. Many plots drawn with ggplot2 come instead from its extension packages: separate packages that add new plots to ggplot2. gog already draws several of these plots: the node-link diagram in Network, the alluvial diagram in Flow, the beeswarm, ridgelines, a halo behind a label, and animation with play. Other extension plots need a computation first, then an ordinary drawing. The host language computes the tables, and gog draws them with words it already has. Five of these plots follow.

F.4.1 Stars from a significance test

Packages such as ggsignif and ggpubr mark a plot with the result of a statistical test. The test is a computation, so it belongs to the host language, which builds two small tables from its result. One holds the corners of each bracket: a line that joins the two groups compared, with a short leg down at each end. The other holds the stars that stand for each p-value. Here a t-test compares the petal length of versicolor, and then of virginica, with that of setosa, the group the other two are compared with:

“Given the iris flowers: boxes, x is species, y is petal length, and also paths from the petal brackets, grouped by pair, and also text from the petal stars, label by stars.”

Each bracket is four rows, which path joins in order: up from setosa, across, and down to the species compared. group(pair) keeps the two brackets apart. The stars sit above the species compared, and three stars mean a p-value below 0.001.

F.4.4 How sets overlap

F.4.5 A horizon chart

A horizon chart draws a long time series in a panel of small height. Each value is measured from one chosen value, here the mean. Differences above it rise from the baseline in shades of one color. Differences below it are turned upward too, in shades of a second color. Each band holds a slice of fixed height, and the part of a difference that does not fit is drawn again from the baseline, in a darker shade, over the lighter band. Packages such as ggHoriPlot draw it. Cutting each difference into slices is simple arithmetic, so the host language does it. This table holds New York’s daily high temperature from May to September 1973, in degrees Fahrenheit, from R’s airquality data. Each row holds one day and one band: the part of that day’s distance from the mean that falls in the band, with three bands above the mean and three below:
